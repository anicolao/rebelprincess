# Zombie Princess variant rules

Status: the first manually playable implementation is available behind the room-level Zombie Princess selection. It supports individual play with the recommended A–D round set, all ten individual-play Princesses, bidding, Prince trump/breaking, the Frog decision, skull purchases, overtricks, four-round scoring, and reconnect through the shared event stream. Team play, Alice, Sleeping Beauty, and round cards E–Z remain follow-up implementation work after the first playtest.

This document follows the current 32-page deluxe rulebook (version 5.3.1) as written. It paraphrases the rules rather than replacing the published rulebook.

## Game at a glance

Zombie Princess is a 3–6 player trick-taking game derived from Spades. Players predict how many tricks they will take, use a Princess power, and manage skulls across four rounds. Princes are the trump suit. A special Frog lets the winner decide whether one captured trick counts. The game supports individual play and fixed teams.

The physical game contains:

- 60 playing cards in four suits, numbered 1–15: Villagers, Pets, Knights, and Princes. The Frog is the special Pet 6 within this deck;
- 12 Princesses;
- 26 round cards plus a reference card;
- 16 bidding coins, 35 skull tokens, and a score pad.

## Modes and setup

- Individual play supports 3–6 players.
- Team play supports four players in two opposing pairs or six players in three opposing pairs. Partners sit opposite/furthest apart.
- With three players, remove ranks 10–15. With four players, remove ranks 11–15. Five and six players use the full ranked deck.
- Deal two Princesses to each player. Each player secretly chooses one and all reveal together. In six-player individual play, deal one Princess each. Team-only Princesses are excluded from individual games.
- Choose four round cards. The first three are ordinary rounds and the fourth is a black-hourglass catch-up round. The rulebook recommends cards A–D for a first game.
- Choose the first leader randomly. The game lasts exactly four rounds.
- Every player begins with 2 skulls.

## Round sequence

1. Shuffle and deal the available suited deck, then reveal and read the round card.
2. Perform its simultaneous card pass or exchange. In team play, exchange with a partner; in individual play, pass in the printed direction. Choose outgoing cards before looking at incoming cards.
3. Bid clockwise from the leader. Take one bidding coin for every trick predicted. Ordinary team bids are combined; zero bids remain individual.
4. Play every trick, applying Princess, Frog, and round effects.
5. Score the round. Return bidding coins, keep skulls, and refresh once-per-round Princess powers.
6. The lowest-scoring player or team chooses whether they or a teammate acts first or last in the next round. Ties resolve clockwise from the previous lowest scorer.

## Tricks, trump, and the Frog

- The leader plays any legal card. Other players must follow the led suit when able and may otherwise play any card.
- The highest card of the led suit wins unless at least one Prince was played. Princes are trump, so the highest Prince wins.
- A Prince cannot be led until a Prince has been discarded off-suit. This “breaks out” the Princes. A player holding only Princes may lead one before that happens.
- The trick winner leads the next trick.
- A player who wins a trick containing the Frog keeps that trick face up. At round end, that player decides whether the trick counts toward their bid. This can preserve a zero bid when their only captured trick contains the Frog.

When effects conflict, resolve Princess powers first, the Frog second, and the round rule third. Simultaneous effects resolve clockwise from the leader’s left.

## Bidding, skulls, and scoring

After the tricks are complete, score in this order:

1. Gain skulls granted by Princess powers. An unused Princess with the printed skull reminder also grants one skull.
2. Score a successful zero bid: 5 points per successful individual zero bid in teams; 10 points in three- or four-player individual games; 5 points in five- or six-player individual games.
3. A player or team short of its bid may buy missing tricks: 3 skulls per trick in individual play or 4 per trick in team play.
4. Apply the current round card’s skull adjustments.
5. If tricks won are at least the bid after those choices, score points equal to the bid.
6. Pay for overtricks: 1 skull per extra trick in individual play or 2 skulls per extra trick in teams. If there are not enough skulls, return all available skulls.

After round four, add all round points and one point for every two remaining skulls, rounded down. Highest score wins; tied players compare remaining skulls, then use the publisher’s online tie-breaker.

Black-hourglass effects compare the current subtotal plus one point per two skulls to determine the temporary leader. That comparison does not itself change final scoring.

## Princess powers

Unless noted as always active, a Princess power is used once per round and the Princess is flipped to show it is exhausted.

| Princess | Power | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | Tea(m) Time — teams only | On your turn, ask your partner to play a card in front of you. Give them one card in return. Unless they already acted, they still take their own turn later in the trick. |
| Cinderella | Smashing Pumpkins — always active | When Princes are led, you do not have to follow with a Prince. |
| Dorothy | Drop a House On It | Immediately put a trick you just won beneath Dorothy. It does not count toward your bid or round effects. Gain 3 skulls during scoring. |
| Goldilocks | This Bid Is Just Right — always active | Gain 3 skulls for matching your bid exactly with your own tricks. Buying tricks with skulls does not earn the bonus. |
| Ice Princess | Flurry Fury | Before a trick, return one bid coin to reduce your bid by one, including to zero. |
| Pea Princess | Pea Shooter | Add 5 to the value of your played card for this trick. It wins ties, but round effects still see its printed value. |
| Rapunzel | A Brush with Death | When you win a trick containing Villagers, exhaust Rapunzel. Gain one skull per Villager during scoring; the trick still counts. |
| Red Riding Hood | Axe-cessorize | At the start of your turn, inspect one previously won trick and swap any card from your hand for a non-Prince in it. Inspecting the trick exhausts the power even if you decline the swap. |
| Scheherazade | The Pen Is Mightier | Before any card is played in the fourth trick, change your bid. Changing it to zero makes it a zero bid. |
| Sleeping Beauty | Spindle of Fight — teams only | Before a trick, give your partner one to three cards. They return the same number. |
| Swan Maiden | 2 Birds 1 Skull — always active | Gain one skull for every two tricks won. |
| Thumbelina | Choose Your Weapon | Play any non-Prince card, ignoring the requirement to follow suit. |

The printed pass/exchange instruction is part of every round card in addition to the effect below. “Half” means half the dealt hand. In team play, players exchange the printed number with their partner instead of passing in the printed direction.

## Interface and engine implications

The existing Rebel Princess table can remain the visual and networking foundation, but the variant cannot be represented as a different deck alone. It needs explicit variant-owned rules and phases:

- a lobby-level game selection and variant identifier stored with every room;
- individual/team seating, partner visibility, and shared team bids;
- a pre-round pass followed by a clockwise bidding phase;
- bid coins, persistent skulls, overtrick costs, trick purchases, and a four-round score sheet;
- 15 ranks, four Zombie suits, Prince-breaking state, and the Frog’s deferred count decision;
- once-per-round, always-active, and team-only Princess capabilities;
- round effects capable of exposing hands, creating private subsets, changing turn eligibility, producing unequal hand sizes, or replacing trick ownership;
- a final-round leaderboard snapshot used by black-hourglass rules;
- replay events that record every private choice and its public consequence without leaking hidden information to other clients.

The safest implementation boundary is a common table shell around two variant rule engines. Shared concepts—room membership, seats, hands, tricks, replays, responsive layout, and asset rendering—stay common. Deck construction, legal actions, phases, effects, scoring, and bot observations belong to the selected variant.

## Questions to settle before implementation

- Decide whether the UI exposes the rulebook’s optional online tie-breaker or ends with a shared win after score and skull ties.
- Validate scoring against the physical edition in hand if its reference card differs from rulebook v5.3.1. This design intentionally follows the current rulebook’s “bid at most tricks won” wording and its separate overtrick penalty.
- Keep all art as replaceable original work. The complete gallery is an art-direction proposal, not a claim to reproduce the publisher’s illustrations.
